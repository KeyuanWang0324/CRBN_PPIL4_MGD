# Novelty and Significance Audit: Evidence-Calibrated Multidimensional Drug-Performance Prediction

## Which components are common versus potentially novel?

### Takeaway
The current core—learning endpoint-specific combinations of docking scores, interaction/structural features, ligand descriptors, and experimental labels—is an established target-specific machine-learning scoring/QSAR pattern, not a new method by itself. A potentially original project emerges if it is reframed as a rigorous, failure-aware test of the **incremental value and transferability of structural information**, with paper/scaffold holdouts, uncertainty calibration, and a mechanistically explicit transition from affinity to molecular-glue degradation.

### Cited Findings
- Consensus scoring has long combined multiple docking/scoring outputs; one study compared eight docking programs over 21 DUD-E targets and found traditional consensus methods more robust than individual methods, with mixture-model and gradient-boosting consensus improving further. The authors explicitly describe mean, median, extreme-rank, voting, mixture-model, and learned weighted-ensemble schemes. — [Palacio-Rodríguez et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5872818/)
- A current practical protocol already describes the workflow of collecting target-specific experimental data, partitioning it into training and test sets, and training target-specific machine-learning scoring functions. — [Adeshina, Deeds and Karanicolas, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41596-023-00885-w)
- Combining structure-based interaction features with ligand-only physicochemical/topological descriptors has direct precedent; ligand-enhanced random forests outperformed structure-only counterparts on PDBbind/CASF benchmarks, while ligand-only features were themselves predictive of average affinities. — [Boyles et al., 2020](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/36/3/758/5554651)
- Target-specific structure-based models are also established. A PARP1 study compared five supervised algorithms using protein–ligand interaction fingerprints and ligand-only features and reported a target-specific SVM/P­LEC model outperforming its classical scoring baseline on its hardest test set. — [Target-specific PARP1 study, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38582911/)
- Multiparameter desirability scores are not new: QED converts several molecular properties into desirability functions and combines them into a weighted geometric mean; CNS MPO similarly integrates six physicochemical properties and was evaluated on marketed drugs, candidates, and a proprietary diversity set. — [Bickerton et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524573/); [Wager et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3368654/)
- Machine learning for degradation is already active. DeepPROTACs used structures of the PROTAC, target pocket, and E3 pocket to classify degradation from DC50/Dmax labels, while a later open model incorporated pDC50, Dmax, E3 identity, target sequence, and cell type; its performance fell substantially when generalizing to novel targets. — [DeepPROTACs, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9681730/); [Ribes et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02637)
- Molecular-glue datasets have recently become materially larger. MolGlueDB reports 1,629 distinct experimentally supported MGDs from 241 publications, with binding, degradation, biological, structural, and physicochemical annotations; MGDB separately reports 7,396 curated molecular glues and 9,728 experimental bioactivity points, although the unit of independence and completeness of comparable endpoints still require audit. — [MolGlueDB, 2026](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1510/8239508); [MGDB, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807674/)
- MolGlueDB explicitly includes inactive MGD candidates, DC50, Dmax, reported degradation percentages, binding measurements from heterogeneous assay formats, structural data, PK/PD, proteomics, and calculated physicochemical properties. This makes a retrospective MGD analysis more feasible than it was previously, but also reveals extensive endpoint heterogeneity. — [MolGlueDB, 2026](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1510/8239508)
- CASF-2016 formalizes distinct evaluation tasks—scoring power, ranking power, docking power, and screening power—showing why one undifferentiated “drug performance score” is methodologically inappropriate. — [Su et al., 2019](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jcim.8b00545)

### Inferences
- **Common/low-novelty components:** training regression or classification on experimental affinity/activity; combining Vina/docking terms with RDKit descriptors; feature-importance-derived “weights”; consensus scoring; QED/ADME aggregation; applying random forest/XGBoost to PDBbind or a single target; predicting DC50/Dmax from degrader records.
- **Potentially novel combination:** use a preregistered hierarchy of feature families and negative controls to ask whether docking/ternary-structure features contribute information that cannot be recovered from ligand identity, target identity, paper/assay context, or simple chemical similarity; then test how that incremental information changes from binary binding to degradation.
- The most defensible novelty is not “a better weighted score,” but a **transportability and failure-decomposition study**: quantify which signal survives (i) new scaffold, (ii) new publication/assay, (iii) new target, and (iv) transfer from affinity to degradation.
- A multidimensional report card is more scientifically valid than one overall score, but it is not novel merely because it has several dimensions. Originality must reside in the validation design, mechanistic features, uncertainty treatment, or a prospective PPIL4 test.
- Current-framing creativity rating: **3/10 at the professional-method level; 5/10 for a high-school project.** The integration is sensible, but most pieces already exist and “learn weights from past compounds” closely resembles standard QSAR/ML scoring.
- Current-framing significance rating: **4/10.** It could organize candidate selection, but a heterogeneous retrospective model without rigorous controls would likely reproduce literature and dataset biases rather than improve biological prediction.

### Gaps
- Database headline counts do not reveal how many independent CRBN MGD compounds have directly comparable, numerical DC50 and Dmax values under sufficiently documented cell line, time, concentration, and assay conditions. This must be audited before committing to an MGD regression endpoint.
- No reliable source found establishing that the exact proposed combination—interpretable feature-family ablation, calibrated uncertainty, strict publication/scaffold holdout, and prospective PPIL4 ranking—has already been performed for CRBN molecular glues. Absence from this search is not proof of novelty.

## What would constitute a meaningful methodological or biological contribution?

### Takeaway
A meaningful contribution would demonstrate, on genuinely out-of-distribution tests, that a defined feature family improves a specified endpoint beyond strong ligand-only and context-only baselines, with calibrated uncertainty and interpretable failure modes. The strongest biological contribution would be prospective enrichment of experimentally active PPIL4 degraders—not a retrospective fit or a visually plausible ternary pose.

### Cited Findings
- DUD-E can produce misleadingly high performance: receptor–ligand and ligand-only CNNs both averaged about 0.98 AUC, and their AUCs were nearly identical, indicating analogue and decoy bias rather than learned protein–ligand physics. The authors recommend avoiding DUD-E for ML method development or applying stringent controls. — [Chen et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6701836/)
- More recent interrogation of ML binding-affinity models found that baseline models based on protein and ligand bias could equal or outperform five popular ML scoring functions on CASF-2016, reinforcing that benchmark performance does not establish meaningful structural learning. — [Volkov et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11821266/)
- PDBbind/CASF overlap can inflate apparent generalization. CleanSplit work reports severe train–test leakage and proposes structure-, protein-, and ligand-aware splitting to obtain a more realistic estimate. — [Li et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01124-5)
- Experimental labels impose a performance ceiling. An analysis of ChEMBL records estimated that mixing Kd, Ki, and IC50 measurements produced MAE 0.78 log units and RMSE 1.04 log units, and concluded that leading PDBbind benchmark results can be overoptimistic relative to experimental uncertainty. — [Hernández et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ailsci.2023.100087)
- IC50 is condition-dependent and cannot generally be converted directly into binding free energy; one leak-proof PDBbind curation therefore created a stricter level retaining exact Kd or Ki records and noted that repeated IC50 measurements can vary by about an order of magnitude. — [Li et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10462179.1/)
- Blinded D3R studies show that receptor choice materially affects predictions; in several challenges even strong strategies achieved affinity-ranking Spearman correlations around 0.5, and choosing a suboptimal receptor undermined rankings. — [Wingert et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5771500/)
- For targeted degradation, a stable/high-affinity ternary complex is not sufficient: inaccessible surface lysines can prevent productive ubiquitination, and linker/ligand geometry, solubility, permeability, and bioavailability influence degradation. — [Roy et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10344917/)
- Mechanistic modeling further indicates that very high cooperativity can sometimes slow catalytic turnover; the relation among affinity, cooperativity, ternary complex abundance, and degradation is therefore non-monotonic and context-dependent. — [Han, Miah and Zorba, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10125322/)
- Uncertainty quantification is an active research area rather than an optional cosmetic layer; a recent study compared deep ensembles, Monte Carlo dropout, Laplace approximation, Bayesian neural networks, and evidential methods on leak-resistant and external affinity sets, noting that uncertainty work remains sparse in binding-affinity prediction. — [UQ comparison study, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12678490/)
- Official ISEF judging criteria give separate weight to research question, methodology, execution/data interpretation, and creativity/potential impact, and explicitly emphasize reproducibility, appropriate statistical methods, sufficient data, limitations, and student understanding. — [Society for Science judging criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)

### Inferences
- A **methodological contribution** requires all of the following: a fixed endpoint; leakage-resistant splits; competitive baselines; feature-family and negative-control ablations; repeated/group-aware uncertainty; and an external or temporally held-out test. A few percentage points under random cross-validation would not qualify.
- The primary scientific hypothesis should be sharpened to: **“Under scaffold- and publication-held-out evaluation, do pose-dependent interaction and ensemble-robustness features improve prediction of a defined experimental endpoint beyond ligand-only chemical similarity and assay-context baselines, and is the improvement retained when moving from binary affinity to CRBN-mediated degradation?”**
- A useful secondary hypothesis is: **“Prediction disagreement and out-of-domain distance can identify compounds for which the model should abstain, reducing high-confidence false positives relative to docking-score ranking.”** This makes uncertainty testable through interval coverage, selective-risk curves, or error-versus-confidence analysis.
- A compelling biological contribution would be a blinded/prospective test: pre-register the ranking and confidence intervals for PPIL4 candidates, test a selected top/bottom/diverse subset, and measure enrichment, DC50, and Dmax. Without wet-lab results, the contribution remains a computational benchmark and prioritization tool.
- A negative result can still be significant if it rigorously shows that structural scores add no transportable information after controlling for ligand and assay context. That conclusion would directly warn against overinterpreting docking in molecular-glue discovery.
- Refined-project creativity rating: **7/10 at ISEF level; 5–6/10 at professional-method level.** The algorithms are not novel, but the controlled question, degradation transfer test, interpretable ablations, and prospective PPIL4 application form a distinctive research design.
- Refined-project significance rating: **7/10 if it includes an external/prospective test and calibrated abstention; 5/10 if it remains retrospective only.** A successful result could improve which compounds are tested, but it would not solve molecular-glue design or establish drug efficacy.

### Gaps
- No current evidence can show that the model will generalize to PPIL4, because PPIL4-specific degradation labels are not yet available at useful scale; this is precisely why PPIL4 should be an external prospective case rather than part of model training.
- Prediction intervals from conformal methods rely on exchangeability or related calibration assumptions; distribution shifts across targets and assay systems may invalidate nominal coverage and must be empirically checked rather than promised.

## How significant would a successful result be, and what claims would be unjustified?

### Takeaway
Success could justify a narrower claim that a reproducible, endpoint-specific ensemble improves ranking or error detection within a defined applicability domain. It would not justify claims about clinical efficacy, general “drug performance,” causal importance of learned weights, or universal transfer from conventional binding to molecular-glue degradation.

### Cited Findings
- D3R prospective challenges found top affinity-ranking correlations only around 0.5 and emphasized that receptor selection and pocket type alter which strategy works best. This establishes a realistic benchmark: modest but reproducible improvements can be useful, but high retrospective correlations deserve skepticism. — [Wingert et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5771500/)
- Consensus-score authors explicitly state that their outputs are ranking scores for enrichment, not predictions of binding energy. — [Palacio-Rodríguez et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5872818/)
- MolGlueDB distinguishes binding affinities, degradation potency, antiproliferative activity, PK/PD, and proteomics as separate data types rather than a single performance label. — [MolGlueDB, 2026](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1510/8239508)
- A PROTAC model that performed well in easier settings fell near 0.60 ROC AUC when tested on novel targets, illustrating that within-dataset performance does not establish target transfer. — [Ribes et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02637)

### Inferences
- Justified after a strong retrospective result: “On pre-specified scaffold/paper-held-out data for target X, the combined model improved ranking/MAE/early enrichment over Vina, nearest-neighbor, and ligand-only baselines, and its uncertainty identified unreliable predictions.”
- Justified after prospective PPIL4 tests: “The pre-registered model enriched active degraders among this tested PPIL4 panel relative to the named baselines.” The claim must report the small sample size and selection procedure.
- Unjustified: “The model predicts drug performance,” “the learned weight proves this feature causes degradation,” “better docking means stronger degradation,” “the framework is universal across targets,” “the compounds will work in vivo,” or “the model replaces wet-lab validation.”
- Feature importance or a regression coefficient is **predictive association**, not mechanistic causation. Correlated score terms make individual weights unstable; bootstrap distributions and grouped feature importance are more honest than a single definitive weight table.
- Even excellent prediction of Kd/Ki addresses target engagement, not permeability, exposure, toxicity, selectivity, ubiquitination competence, proteasomal processing, or therapeutic effect. Endpoints should remain separate until a decision rule is explicitly defined.

### Gaps
- The practical value of any improvement depends on prospective decision consequences: how many compounds can be tested, baseline hit rate, cost per test, and whether false negatives or false positives are more costly. Those PPIL4-specific quantities are not yet established.

## What prototype target and data design best balance feasibility, analogy to degraders, and originality?

### Takeaway
No conventional inhibitor target is mechanistically analogous enough to validate transfer to molecular glues. Use a conventional target only to validate the statistical workflow, preferably a blinded D3R target with multiple structures and a clean affinity endpoint; then retrain—not simply transfer the same weights—on CRBN MGD data and reserve PPIL4 as an external prospective test.

### Cited Findings
- D3R provides blinded pose and affinity tasks. HSP90 and MAP4K4 were included in the 2015 challenge and FXR in the subsequent challenge; performance differed between deep, well-defined and open/flexible pockets, making the sets useful for testing receptor-choice robustness. — [Wingert et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5771500/)
- A cross-docking benchmark of 949 ligands across ten extensively studied targets includes carbonic anhydrase II, CDK2, HIV protease, thrombin, BACE1, HSP90α, MAPK14, PPARγ, PTP1B, and HIV reverse transcriptase, documenting the availability of many ligand-bound structures for these systems. — [Jain, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4464052/)
- LIT-PCBA was designed from high-confidence PubChem assays and contains experimentally confirmed actives and inactives across 15 target sets; it was explicitly proposed for virtual-screening and ML evaluation, although some target sets have few actives and it is primarily a classification/enrichment resource rather than a clean quantitative-affinity collection. — [Tran-Nguyen et al., 2020](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jcim.0c00155); [LIT-PCBA project site](https://drugdesign.unistra.fr/LIT-PCBA/)
- Time-split validation was proposed to better estimate prospective performance than random splits in drug discovery. — [Sheridan, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23521722/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended prototype:** HSP90 using a D3R-style historical/blinded separation, because it is structurally well characterized, has a defined pocket, has multiple holo structures, and has published prospective-style challenge results. It tests whether the pipeline works under favorable conditions without pretending to model degradation.
- **Recommended stress test:** MAP4K4 or FXR, because their more open/flexible pockets make receptor dependence and ensemble uncertainty visible. A two-target “easy pocket versus flexible/open pocket” design is scientifically stronger and more original than a single CDK2/PDBbind random split.
- **Feasible alternative:** CDK2 with exact Ki/Kd records, multiple co-crystals, a strict time/publication/scaffold split, and held-out receptor conformations. It has abundant structural data, but kinase-series bias and assay heterogeneity must be managed, and it is not biologically closer to CRBN degradation.
- **Classification alternative:** use one LIT-PCBA target such as ALDH1 for active/inactive screening with experimentally confirmed inactives. This is useful for early enrichment, but should not be mixed with quantitative affinity prediction and may require docking an impractically large library unless a preregistered subset is used.
- Do **not** use broad PDBbind random splitting as the central prototype; that would read as a reproduction of a crowded benchmark and be vulnerable to leakage. PDBbind can supply training data only if cleaned and split by protein, ligand scaffold, interaction similarity, publication, or time as appropriate.
- The MGD stage should be trained separately on CRBN-recruiting compounds from MolGlueDB/MGDB, with endpoint-specific subsets: degradation active/inactive; pDC50 where numerical values and censoring are usable; Dmax; selectivity if sufficient labels exist. Conventional-target weights should initialize a hypothesis at most, not be frozen and presented as biologically transferable.
- A particularly sharp design is **nested transfer auditing**: (A) within-scaffold random control, (B) new scaffold, (C) new publication/assay, (D) new neosubstrate, and (E) PPIL4 prospective test. Report the performance decay at each step as a result in its own right.

### Gaps
- D3R dataset licensing/access and the exact number of usable numerical labels per target should be confirmed before final target selection.
- A data audit is needed to quantify complete-case CRBN MGD records and identify whether multiple measurements of the same compound–target pair can support hierarchical modeling rather than naive deduplication.

## What baseline comparisons and ablations are essential?

### Takeaway
The study is only credible if the combined model beats simple chemical-similarity, ligand-only, context-only, single-score, and naive-consensus baselines under identical grouped splits. Ablations must demonstrate that pose-dependent information—not leakage or compound identity—is responsible for any claimed gain.

### Cited Findings
- A ligand-only CNN achieved nearly the same DUD-E performance as a receptor–ligand CNN; replacing receptor information with a dummy atom barely changed results, demonstrating why ligand-only and receptor-removal controls are essential. — [Chen et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6701836/)
- CASF separates scoring, ranking, docking, and screening power; therefore a model should be evaluated on metrics matched to its intended use, rather than a single correlation. — [Su et al., 2019](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jcim.8b00545)
- Standard virtual-screening metrics include ROC-AUC, precision–recall AUC, and early enrichment such as EF1%; consensus-scoring work also used statistical comparison over targets. — [Palacio-Rodríguez et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5872818/)
- LIT-PCBA was created because benchmark composition and decoy bias can make virtual-screening results unrealistically easy; it emphasizes experimentally confirmed inactive compounds. — [Tran-Nguyen et al., 2020](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jcim.0c00155)

### Inferences
- **Minimum baselines:** experimental-label mean/majority class; molecular-weight/logP rules; nearest-neighbor by ECFP Tanimoto; regularized linear model on 2D descriptors; random forest or gradient boosting on ligand-only fingerprints; Vina or the chosen docking score alone; each structural metric alone; unweighted mean and rank consensus; one published generic ML scoring function if reproducible.
- **MGD-specific baselines:** compound-only; target/neosubstrate-only; E3-only; assay-context-only (cell line, exposure time, concentration/assay type); compound + context without structure; binary CRBN-binding proxy without ternary features. A combined model must beat these to support a structural-mechanistic claim.
- **Feature-family ablations:** ligand descriptors/fingerprints; raw docking scores; protein–ligand contact fingerprints; pose quality/clashes/strain; receptor-ensemble stability and docking variance; ternary-interface geometry; accessible-lysine features; predicted ADME; assay context; uncertainty/OOD distance. Remove one group at a time and also add groups sequentially to a ligand-only base.
- **Negative controls:** shuffle labels within appropriate groups; replace the receptor with a dummy or irrelevant receptor; randomize/perturb poses; permute docking scores within scaffold; use receptor identity without coordinates; compare crystal-pose rescoring with docked-pose scoring. If performance survives destructive pose controls, it cannot be credited to learned structural recognition.
- **Validation:** nested cross-validation for tuning; repeated scaffold-group splits; publication-held-out and preferably temporal tests; all records of one compound/scaffold/publication kept in one fold; a final untouched external set; bootstrap confidence intervals over groups rather than individual duplicate measurements.
- **Regression metrics:** MAE/RMSE with experimental-noise context, Spearman/Kendall for prioritization, and per-scaffold/per-paper errors. **Classification/ranking metrics:** PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, EF1% or top-k precision, sensitivity/specificity, and decision-curve or expected-utility analysis if a testing budget is defined. **Calibration:** Brier score/reliability curves for probabilities and empirical coverage/width for prediction intervals.
- **Interpretable weighting:** use standardized elastic-net regression or an additive/generalized additive model as the primary “weighting” model, reporting bootstrap coefficient stability. Compare against nonlinear tree boosting as a challenger. If nonlinear performance is better, do not force it into a misleading single weight table; use grouped permutation importance or SHAP cautiously as descriptive, not causal.
- Pre-register the primary endpoint, split, metric, and success criterion. One reasonable success criterion is a statistically supported improvement over the strongest ligand-only baseline on the untouched group-held-out set, plus acceptable calibration—not merely superiority to Vina.

### Gaps
- The minimum detectable improvement and required sample size cannot be specified until the number of independent compounds and groups, endpoint variance, and expected baseline performance are known.
- SHAP and permutation importance can be unstable with correlated descriptors; the appropriate stability threshold should be selected only after inspecting feature correlations and effective sample size.
