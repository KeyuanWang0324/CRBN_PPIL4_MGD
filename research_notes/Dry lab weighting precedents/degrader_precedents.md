# Predictive and Multimodal Models for Targeted Protein Degraders

## What databases and predictive models exist for PROTAC degradation, ternary-complex formation, DC50/Dmax, or molecular-glue activity?

### Takeaway
There is now a substantial computational ecosystem for degrader prediction, but it is fragmented by endpoint. Public databases contain thousands of PROTAC or molecular-glue records, while most predictive models address either binary degradation activity, continuous DC50/Dmax, recruitment, or ternary-complex geometry—not all dimensions together.

### Cited Findings
- PROTAC-DB 3.0 (2025) reports 6,111 PROTACs, 569 warheads, 2,753 linkers, 107 E3 ligands, and 959 target–PROTAC–E3 ternary-complex structures; it also added pharmacokinetic data. This is a primary public source for PROTAC model training, but its records are not equivalent to uniformly measured assays. — [PROTAC-DB 3.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39225044/)
- TPDdb (2026) reports 22,183 PROTACs and 6,002 molecular glues, together with 27,796 activity records such as IC50, DC50, and Dmax across 201 cell lines. Its authors explicitly state that curated inactive/negative data are a future addition, showing that a large molecule count does not solve the negative-data problem. — [TPDdb](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1683/8285777)
- MolGlueDB (2025/2026) is a dedicated degradative-molecular-glue database assembled from 241 publications. It contains 1,840 entries representing 1,629 distinct MGDs, 28 recruiting proteins, and 94 targets, with chemical structures, affinities, degradation measurements, biological activity, physicochemical properties, structural data, proteomics, and PK/PD annotations. It includes candidates reported inactive against the intended substrate, which can support negative-label construction, although an “inactive” compound may still degrade an untested target. — [MolGlueDB](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1510/8239508)
- DeepPROTACs (2022) was an early supervised neural model. It labeled PROTACs as good degraders when DC50 <100 nM and Dmax >80%, used POI/E3 structural-pocket and degrader representations, and reported 11/16 correct predictions in a small experimental test. Its endpoint was binary degradation rather than continuous, multidimensional performance. — [DeepPROTACs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9681730/)
- Ribes et al. (2024) combined PROTAC fingerprints, POI and E3 information, and cell-type embeddings to predict a binary label derived from pDC50 and Dmax. Reported performance declined from 82.6% accuracy/0.848 ROC-AUC in the best test setting to 61%/0.615 when generalizing to novel protein targets, illustrating the gap between interpolation and true transfer. — [Modeling PROTAC degradation activity with machine learning](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/542045)
- PROTAC-STAN (2025) represents the molecule at atom, molecule, and property levels, encodes POI and E3 proteins with structure-informed protein-language representations, and learns interactions through ternary attention. It is a close example of automatically learned multimodal weighting, but still predicts a binary degradation endpoint rather than a transparent panel of docking, structural, cellular, and ADME outcomes. — [PROTAC-STAN paper and code](https://github.com/PROTACs/PROTAC-STAN)
- DegradeMaster (2025) combines 3D molecular graphs, an E(3)-equivariant encoder, selected chemical descriptors, mutual-attention pooling, and semisupervised pseudolabeling. It used 1,503 labeled entries and 8,636 total entries from PROTAC-DB variants; its authors acknowledge that the binary high/low label is arbitrary and propose future DC50/Dmax regression. — [DegradeMaster](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12261415/)
- SE(3)-PROTACs (2026) combines SE(3)-equivariant 3D PROTAC substructure graphs, ESM protein embeddings, and a learned pairwise interaction mechanism. On 1,979 curated records, accuracy fell from 80.81% on a random split to 65.62% on a cold-target cluster split and 64.08% on a temporal split; this is direct evidence that realistic validation is substantially harder. — [SE(3)-PROTACs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13158127/)
- A hybrid deep-learning/QSP model (2026) is currently the closest published PROTAC analogue to an evidence-weighting system: it combines predicted binary binding affinities, protein turnover, physicochemical descriptors, and a mechanistic Hook model, then learns correction coefficients to predict DC50 and Dmax. On 444 measurements for 272 PROTACs and 53 POIs, it reported test R2 = 0.547 for DC50 but R2 = -0.180 for Dmax. — [Deep-learning–QSP PROTAC efficacy model](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-026-01152-2)
- DeepTernary (2025) addresses a different endpoint: ternary-complex structure. It trained an SE(3)-equivariant model on 22,303 generic PDB ternary complexes after excluding similar known PROTAC/MGD complexes, then evaluated zero-shot on degrader complexes. It achieved average DockQ 0.65 for a 22-structure PROTAC benchmark but only 0.21 on the more difficult MGD benchmark, reinforcing that structural accuracy and degradation efficacy are separate problems. — [DeepTernary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11888550/)
- A 2024 benchmark of PRosettaC, MOE, and ICM found that all three can generate experimentally observed PROTAC ternary geometries but also many structures far from the crystal reference; molecular dynamics suggested multiple configurational states and raised doubts about treating one experimental structure as a unique ground truth. — [Rovers and Schapira benchmark](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00426)

### Inferences
- “Learned weights” already exist implicitly through neural attention, feature fusion, SHAP-ranked descriptors, or learned QSP correction coefficients; therefore, simply combining several scores with optimized coefficients is not by itself a novel contribution. The novel unit would need to be the endpoint design, validation protocol, uncertainty calibration, mechanistic decomposition, or an unusually reproducible benchmark. — [PROTAC-STAN](https://github.com/PROTACs/PROTAC-STAN); [Deep-learning–QSP model](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-026-01152-2)
- The strongest defensible output is a vector of predictions—such as ternary recruitment, DC50, Dmax, cellular-context robustness, selectivity, and confidence—rather than one “drug performance” score, because current papers show that even DC50 and Dmax have different predictability. — [Deep-learning–QSP model](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-026-01152-2)

### Gaps
- No open, peer-reviewed model located in this search jointly learns from explicit docking scores, ternary-interface scores, physicochemical/ADME features, omics context, and standardized degradation outcomes while returning a calibrated multidimensional profile.
- Many current models use learned embeddings rather than interpretable numerical weights, so direct scientific interpretation of “how much docking matters” remains incomplete.

## Are there CRBN-specific or molecular-glue-specific retrospective benchmarks, and is molecular-glue data scarcity still a reason not to use MGDs as the first prototype?

### Takeaway
CRBN-specific molecular-glue modeling now exists, and new public databases make a prototype more feasible than it was several years ago. Nevertheless, an MGD-first prototype remains substantially higher risk than a conventional inhibitor or even a PROTAC prototype because the effective sample size collapses after controlling for target, scaffold, assay, publication, and cell context, and because recruitment, ubiquitination, degradation, and cellular response are distinct endpoints.

### Cited Findings
- Prael et al. (2024) trained CRBN-glue proteochemometric models using more than 100 proprietary screening campaigns and thousands of glues. Models combined compound and protein descriptors; 89% of models had ROC-AUC >0.8 and 70% had MCC >0.2, and more than 80% of proteochemometric models outperformed compound-only per-target models. The endpoint was CRBN-mediated recruitment, not necessarily target degradation, and the underlying data are confidential. — [Prael et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667318524000072)
- MG2Act (August 2026 preprint) is a CRBN-specific, structure-independent sequential-attention model that encodes E3 engagement before substrate recruitment. It was built from 1,207 compound–target pairs across 47 targets, refined to 1,159 pairs after excluding rare targets, and reports strict redundancy-filtered evaluation plus prospective identification of nanomolar degraders for IKZF1, CK1alpha, and CDK4. It is currently a preprint, so peer-reviewed confirmation is still pending. — [MG2Act](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.08.739980v1)
- DeepTernary explicitly included an MGD structural benchmark, but its average MGD DockQ of 0.21 was much lower than its PROTAC DockQ of 0.65. The paper notes that only a few dozen experimentally resolved PROTAC/MGD ternary complexes exist, which makes direct structure-supervised learning impractical. — [DeepTernary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11888550/)
- MolGlueDB’s 1,840 records span 94 targets and 28 recruiting proteins and mix binding, degradation, antiproliferation, proteomics, structural, and PK/PD evidence. These records are valuable but are not 1,840 interchangeable measurements of one standardized endpoint. — [MolGlueDB](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1510/8239508)
- TPDdb includes 6,002 molecular glues but explicitly plans to add curated negative data in the future. This indicates that public datasets remain outcome-biased toward reported successes even when their headline molecule counts are large. — [TPDdb](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1683/8285777)
- Degradation readouts are context- and time-dependent. In a published cell-line study, MZ1 DC50 shifted from 29 to 11 nM between 2 and 8 hours in the same H838 cell line, showing why values from different papers cannot be treated as intrinsic constants without assay metadata. — [VHL-PROTAC profiling study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8919295/)

### Inferences
- MGD data are no longer absent; the central problem is *effective, leakage-safe data volume*. A dataset with roughly 1,000 pairs can become much smaller when the evaluation withholds related scaffolds, targets, or publications, making uncertainty large for a new target such as PPIL4. — [MG2Act](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.08.739980v1); [SE(3)-PROTACs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13158127/)
- A CRBN-MGD prototype is defensible only if narrowly scoped—for example, predicting one assay-normalized recruitment or degradation endpoint within CRBN—and if it includes grouped validation by compound series, target, and paper. It is not yet defensible as a universal “real-world drug performance” predictor. — [Prael et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667318524000072); [MG2Act](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.08.739980v1)
- Starting with a data-rich inhibitor target remains methodologically cleaner for demonstrating that score fusion adds value. Starting with PROTACs would be a middle ground because public degradation datasets and benchmark models are larger and more mature, while a later CRBN-glue transfer test would preserve direct relevance to PPIL4. — [PROTAC-DB 3.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39225044/); [MolGlueDB](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1510/8239508)

### Gaps
- The precise overlap, duplicate-compound rate, scaffold diversity, and assay completeness across MolGlueDB, MGDB, and TPDdb have not yet been independently audited.
- The MG2Act full prospective protocol and raw experimental measurements need detailed review before treating its reported prospective success as a validated benchmark.
- No open CRBN-MGD benchmark located here appears to combine explicit ternary docking metrics with matched wet-lab recruitment, DC50, Dmax, selectivity, and cell-context data for the same compound set.

## What is the closest prior work, and how creative and significant is the proposed project?

### Takeaway
The broad concept—learn a combination of molecular, protein, structural, and biological features from retrospective degrader data—is already an active research area. The project can still be meaningfully creative if it is reframed as a transparent, leakage-resistant, uncertainty-calibrated benchmark that tests whether explicit docking/structure evidence adds predictive value beyond simpler chemical and biological baselines, then transfers that framework prospectively to PPIL4.

### Cited Findings
- The closest *mechanistic weighted-model* precedent is the 2026 deep-learning/QSP PROTAC paper: predicted binding affinities and protein turnover enter a Hook model, while a DNN learns coefficients from whole-PROTAC, warhead, ligase-ligand, linker, and physicochemical descriptors. Its moderate DC50 result and failed Dmax generalization show both feasibility and a clear unresolved problem. — [Deep-learning–QSP model](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-026-01152-2)
- The closest *multimodal degradation classifier* precedents are Ribes et al., PROTAC-STAN, DegradeMaster, and SE(3)-PROTACs. They already fuse molecule, protein, 3D, cell-line, or descriptor information with learned weights/attention, so novelty cannot rest on “using several scores instead of one.” — [Ribes et al.](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/542045); [PROTAC-STAN](https://github.com/PROTACs/PROTAC-STAN); [DegradeMaster](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12261415/); [SE(3)-PROTACs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13158127/)
- The closest *CRBN molecular-glue* precedents are Prael et al.’s proprietary recruitment models and MG2Act’s continuous CRBN-MGD predictor. Neither, based on the accessible descriptions, explicitly tests the incremental value of docking and structural-quality scores against carefully controlled nonstructural baselines. — [Prael et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667318524000072); [MG2Act](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.08.739980v1)
- The structural benchmark literature establishes that ternary-model scoring is uncertain and that multiple conformational states may be physically relevant. A project that treats docking scores as noisy evidence and calibrates their contribution against experiment would directly address this documented limitation. — [Rovers and Schapira](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00426)

### Inferences
- **Creativity of the broad idea: moderate-low.** Multimodal learned fusion and degradation prediction already exist in several published systems. A manual literature-derived weighted sum would likely be less novel and less rigorous than these models. — [PROTAC-STAN](https://github.com/PROTACs/PROTAC-STAN); [Deep-learning–QSP model](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-026-01152-2)
- **Creativity of a carefully reframed project: moderate-high.** A transparent “value-of-evidence” study could compare chemistry-only, protein/context-only, docking-only, and combined models under scaffold-, target-, publication-, and temporal-held-out validation; quantify uncertainty; and return separate endpoints rather than one opaque score. The sharp random-to-cold-target performance drop in SE(3)-PROTACs shows why this validation focus is scientifically meaningful. — [SE(3)-PROTACs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13158127/)
- **Scientific significance: high if prospective and leakage-safe; moderate if only retrospective.** A successful prospective prioritization that saves wet-lab experiments would be directly useful. A retrospective random-split accuracy comparison alone would have limited significance because existing models already achieve strong random-split metrics that degrade under harder splits. — [Ribes et al.](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/542045); [SE(3)-PROTACs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13158127/)
- **Significance for the PPIL4 project: potentially high but hypothesis-generating.** The framework could make PPIL4 candidate ranking more principled and expose which evidence dimensions drive each recommendation, but predictions for a new neosubstrate should be presented with wide uncertainty until experimentally tested because CRBN-MGD transfer beyond represented targets remains difficult. — [MG2Act](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.08.739980v1); [Prael et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667318524000072)
- The most defensible prototype sequence is: (1) data-rich conventional protein–ligand affinity to establish the score-fusion and calibration method; (2) public PROTAC degradation data to add cell context and ternary mechanism; (3) CRBN molecular glues as a transfer-learning/limited-data challenge; and (4) PPIL4 as a prospective case study. This sequencing distinguishes method development from biological application and reduces the risk that MGD-specific noise obscures whether the core weighting idea works. — [PROTAC-DB 3.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39225044/); [MolGlueDB](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1510/8239508)

### Gaps
- A formal novelty claim would require a systematic review protocol and full-text comparison of feature sets, data splits, and endpoints for every recent model, particularly fast-moving 2026 preprints.
- It remains unknown whether explicit docking scores add independent predictive signal after modern molecular/protein embeddings are included; this is an excellent testable research question rather than an assumption.
- A power analysis is needed after deduplication and grouped splitting to determine whether the available MGD dataset can support the intended number of predictors without overfitting.
