# Mechanistic Modeling Can Strengthen PPIL4 Validation

**Recommendation: use the 2026 molecular-glue biophysical framework as the project’s mechanistic model, but fit it in stages only after replicated PPIL4 concentration–time data exist; do not use the 2026 deep-learning–QSP model to rank the current PPIL4 compounds.** The directly relevant paper is Chae et al., *Biophysical Modeling Elucidates Mechanistic Principles for Rational Molecular Glue Design*, a mass-action model that includes CRBN-mediated degradation and can connect ternary-complex behavior to time-dependent protein loss ([Chae et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c03125)). The “deep-learning QSP” paper is Goo et al., *Proteolysis-targeting Chimera efficacy prediction using a deep-learning–QSP model*; it is a specific PROTAC “Deep Hook” implementation, not a general molecular-glue predictor ([Goo et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-026-01152-2)). For this ISEF project, the strongest near-term analysis is therefore an empirical DC50/Dmax fit followed by a reduced PPIL4 turnover ODE, with the full Chae model added only when ternary biophysics, protein abundance, and turnover measurements are available. The frozen `ppil4_lock_v1` scores should remain preregistered structural-plausibility evidence and a prospective enrichment hypothesis; they must not be converted into affinity, cooperativity, degradation, or efficacy parameters. Goo’s model can inform reporting, baselines, and validation design, but applying its published network to monovalent PPIL4 glues would create an unvalidated PROTAC-to-glue domain transfer. All simulation, fitting, resampling, and any optional reproduction of the deep model can run locally; the mechanistic ODE work is modest, whereas the published deep-QSP workflow assumes a Docker/CUDA-capable setup ([official Goo repository](https://github.com/swgoo/protac_deep_qsp)).

## The two 2026 models solve different problems

The exact **2026 molecular-glue biophysical model** is the Chae et al. JCIM paper, published online on 13 April 2026. It is not a structure predictor or neural network. Its CRBN degrader module represents free glue, CRBN/effector, protein of interest, binary complexes, and the ternary complex with reversible mass-action reactions, then adds basal target synthesis and turnover plus a lumped ternary-complex-to-degradation rate. In PPIL4 notation, the principal quantities are glue–CRBN affinity, baseline CRBN–PPIL4 affinity, PPIL4 binding to the glue–CRBN complex, thermodynamic cooperativity, CRBN and PPIL4 abundances, basal PPIL4 turnover, and an effective degradation rate. The authors fit parameter **ensembles**, not a single privileged parameter vector, because relative-abundance assays leave correlated kinetic quantities incompletely identified; their public MATLAB implementation includes CRBN degrader fits to BRET/AlphaScreen and iMRM/HiBiT-type data ([Chae et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13126642/); [official Chae repository](https://github.com/jcd111/MG_Manuscript_Model)). A closely related but distinct paper is Couturier et al.’s **2025** thermodynamic framework for extracting binding and cooperativity from two-dimensional biochemical concentration matrices; it is complementary input generation, not the “2026 model” itself ([Couturier et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.5c09166)).

The exact **2026 deep-learning–QSP model** is Goo et al.’s PROTAC model. It combines DeepCalici-predicted binary dissociation constants with a mechanistic Hook equation and a feed-forward network that adjusts mechanistic parameters using full-PROTAC, warhead, linker, E3-ligand, physicochemical, target-turnover, and binding features. Its training set contained **444 measurements for 272 PROTACs, 53 targets, and only CRBN or VHL as E3 ligases**, split 80:20 by measurement rather than by held-out compound, scaffold, or target. Reported test performance was materially different for the two endpoints: **R² = 0.547 for DC50 but R² = −0.180 for Dmax**, with the authors attributing weak Dmax transfer to missing context such as cell line, E3 abundance, proteasome availability, treatment duration, and detection method ([Goo et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12888238/)). “DeepQSP” is therefore a terminology trap: here it means a hybrid PROTAC Hook model, while the same label is also used for an unrelated quorum-sensing-peptide classifier. More importantly, a PPIL4 glue has no separable warhead–linker–E3-ligand architecture, so the published feature schema and learned corrections are not transferable by entering a glue SMILES.

| Decision factor | Chae 2026 molecular-glue model | Goo 2026 deep-learning QSP |
|---|---|---|
| Modality match | **Direct:** includes CRBN glue degradation | **Poor:** built for bifunctional PROTACs |
| Useful inputs | Measured binding/cooperativity, CRBN and PPIL4 abundance, PPIL4 turnover, time-resolved degradation, effective degradation rate | PROTAC component descriptors, two binary affinities, target turnover, training-context features |
| Outputs | Ternary occupancy, PPIL4 remaining over dose/time, apparent potency and maximum loss, sensitivity and bottleneck hypotheses | Predicted PROTAC DC50 and Dmax |
| Data burden | Moderate if fitted first as a reduced ODE; high for full microscopic interpretation | High, because transfer would require a large standardized molecular-glue dataset and retraining |
| Validation needed | Synthetic recovery, profile/ensemble identifiability, held-out time/dose and preferably held-out compound | Grouped compound/scaffold/target validation plus prospective glue data; published random measurement split is insufficient |
| ISEF role | **Core mechanistic extension after direct degradation data** | **Methods precedent or optional reproduction only** |

## Locked docking can prioritize tests but cannot parameterize either model

The project already has a complete, reproducible structural-triage layer: all 27 compounds were processed under ligand-inclusive `ppil4_lock_v1`, and the repository explicitly defines those outputs as structural-plausibility enrichment rather than degradation or efficacy prediction ([project README](../README.md)). The three proposed purchases—RW_1987, RW_465, and RW_1992—have mean best-10 HADDOCK scores of −129.905, −127.583, and −127.205, respectively, compared with −125.610 for Z6466608628, −109.961 for TM-04-064-02, and −105.097 for `negctrl_no_ppil4_arm` ([locked candidate table](../final_buy_list_lock_v1_top20_\(Ryan\).csv); [locked control table](../08_controls_results_\(Ryan\).csv)). These values are useful for a **frozen prospective question**—does the preregistered structural rank enrich direct PPIL4 degraders?—but not as K_D, cooperativity, residence time, ubiquitination rate, DC50, Dmax, or cellular exposure. The repository itself reports that HADDOCK score is size-biased and interface-dominated; the unusually high cluster convergence of TM-04-064-02 likewise means convergence within this protocol, not biological activity.

The present Z6466608628 pilot is valuable for assay development but cannot identify a kinetic model. It contains one plate, technical wells and rereads rather than independent biological replication, row–treatment confounding, and a Western blot with one lane per condition plus saturation/loading concerns; it therefore cannot support a precise DC50, Dmax, hook effect, or fitted `kcat` ([fluorescence analysis](2026-10-07_GFP_Fluorescence_Assay_Analysis.md); [Western/statistical audit](2026-10-08_Z64_Fluorescence_Western_Blot_Statistics_and_Correlation.md)). The external PPIL4 study gives a sound assay anchor: Z6466608628 produced a **biochemical recruitment EC50 of 0.34 µM** and more selective PPIL4 recruitment than FPFT-2216, yet cellular PPIL4 loss remained modest and incomplete even at 10 µM ([Baek et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289921/)). That recruitment EC50 is not a cellular degradation DC50; the gap between recruitment and depletion is exactly the mechanistic problem the Chae framework can interrogate after calibration.

The project should preserve five separate endpoints rather than collapse them into one score. **Structural plausibility** asks whether a candidate can support a compatible CRBN–glue–PPIL4 geometry and is addressed by locked docking. **Recruitment** asks whether glue increases physical CRBN–PPIL4 proximity and requires TR-FRET, BRET, AlphaScreen, or equivalent biophysics. **Degradation** asks how much and how quickly PPIL4 protein is lost and requires direct HiBiT or quantitative Western concentration–time data. **Selectivity** asks which other proteins are recruited or depleted and requires targeted panels or proteomics. **Cellular efficacy and RAS dependence** ask whether matched PPIL4 loss changes splicing and cell behavior preferentially in a RAS-defined context; neither follows from docking, recruitment, nor degradation alone. The 2026 spliceosome-vulnerability result supports testing a RAS hypothesis but does not establish that PPIL4 degradation is RAS-selective ([Wagner et al., 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-71564-z)).

## A reduced ODE should precede full biophysical fitting

The first defensible model is an empirical four-parameter Hill/Emax curve fitted separately by compound, cell line, time, and biological experiment. It should estimate an apparent DC50 only when the sampled concentrations span both shoulders and a Dmax only when a plateau is supported; otherwise the result should be reported as a bound or observed maximum. Once a compact concentration–time dataset exists, a reduced ODE can represent baseline PPIL4 synthesis/turnover plus a concentration-dependent induced-loss term. Basal PPIL4 turnover should be measured independently or tightly constrained, and uncertainty should be reported with multi-start ensembles, profile likelihoods, or bootstrap intervals. This reduced model can estimate **apparent onset, induced-loss, plateau, and recovery behavior** without falsely claiming separate microscopic affinities or catalytic constants.

The full Chae network should be added sequentially rather than fitted all at once. Candidate-specific glue–CRBN K_D requires SPR/BLI/ITC or a validated competition assay. Ternary affinity and cooperativity require a two-dimensional biochemical matrix rather than a one-dimensional cellular dose curve. Cell-line-specific PPIL4 and CRBN starting abundances require calibrated tags, quantitative Western, or targeted proteomics; basal PPIL4 half-life gives `gamma = ln(2)/t½`. Time-resolved HiBiT constrains protein loss, while in-vitro ubiquitination and intracellular exposure help distinguish weak recruitment, inefficient ubiquitin transfer/processing, and poor cell availability. Even then, Chae’s `kcat` remains an **effective lumped ubiquitination-plus-degradation term**, not a uniquely measured molecular constant. An optional free-versus-spliceosome-bound PPIL4 state is biologically motivated because spliceosome-bound PPIL4 may occlude the glue-recruitment surface, but it should be added only if data can identify it ([Baek et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289921/)).

Validation should be prospective within the experiment. The fitting code should first recover identifiable parameters from simulated noisy data and explicitly demonstrate failure to recover confounded parameters. Biological fitting should then leave out complete time points or doses, and preferably one compound, before testing prediction. A meaningful ISEF result would be that the calibrated reduced/full model predicts held-out PPIL4 loss more accurately than a Hill curve or recruitment EC50 alone, or that it correctly identifies a mechanistic ceiling such as limited CRBN abundance or downstream processing. Failure to improve held-out prediction would also be informative. A visually good fit to all available points is not validation, and neither modeling route constitutes wet-lab confirmation.

## Four stages convert measurements into a testable model

| Stage | Minimal experiment and analysis | Decision enabled |
|---|---|---|
| **A — establish direct degradation** | Test Z6466608628, FPFT-2216, `negctrl_no_ppil4_arm`, RW_1987, RW_465, and RW_1992; retain TM-04-064-02 as an optional orthogonal comparator. Use vehicle plus 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, and 10 µM at 6 and 24 h, with at least three independent experiments and technical duplicates/triplicates. Measure validated endogenous PPIL4-HiBiT or unsaturated total-protein-normalized Western, plus matched viability/cell count. Randomize or block plate position, hold DMSO constant, and include no-cell compound controls for optical interference. Fit empirical DC50/Dmax with censoring and QC rules. | Which compounds reproducibly degrade PPIL4, at what observed potency/maximum loss, and whether the locked ranking enriches activity. |
| **B — estimate compact kinetics** | Advance Z6466608628, no more than two active novel candidates, and the no-arm negative. For each, use vehicle plus approximately DC20, DC50, and the highest non-cytotoxic plateau concentration at 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, and 24 h; add 48 h only if needed. Maintain three independent experiments. Optionally expose for 6 h, wash, and measure recovery at 0, 2, 6, 18, and 42 h post-washout. Fit the reduced turnover ODE with ensemble/profile uncertainty and held-out time/dose prediction. | Whether behavior is rapid, delayed, durable, reversible, or plateau-limited, without over-resolving microscopic parameters. |
| **C — identify mechanism and selectivity** | For the best candidate, add CRBN loss/knockdown or rescue and proteasome or cullin-neddylation inhibition with inhibitor-only toxicity controls. Measure a PPIL4–CRBN ternary concentration matrix for affinity/cooperativity, quantify known CRBN neosubstrates alongside PPIL4, and reserve unbiased proteomics for the strongest candidate. Add measured K_D, abundance, turnover, and exposure terms sequentially to the Chae model. | Whether PPIL4 loss is CRBN–CRL4/proteasome dependent, recruitment-limited, processing-limited, exposure-limited, or accompanied by off-target degradation. |
| **D — test RAS dependence downstream** | In an isogenic RAS-on/RAS-off pair, choose sublethal doses that achieve matched PPIL4 degradation. Measure PPIL4 remaining, one prespecified splicing readout, and viability/proliferation in the same experiments; measure CRBN abundance and use a degradation-resistant PPIL4 rescue if feasible. Analyze a RAS-state × treatment interaction while conditioning on measured PPIL4 loss. | Whether equal PPIL4 depletion causes a larger downstream effect in the RAS-on state, rather than merely reflecting different exposure, CRBN level, or degradation depth. |

This design also defines where the deep-learning model belongs. Goo’s paper is a useful **negative-control and reporting precedent**: it demonstrates that DC50 and Dmax must remain separate, that assay context controls maximum degradation, and that random row-level splitting can overstate prospective transfer. Reproducing its public code locally on known PROTAC examples could document these issues, but its outputs should not be merged into the PPIL4 buy-list score. A future neural extension would require hundreds of independent, standardized CRBN molecular-glue compound–condition observations, group-held-out validation by compound/scaffold/publication/target, and an ablation showing that the neural component improves held-out prediction beyond the mechanistic ODE while preserving parameter identifiability. With three recommended candidates plus controls, deep learning is underdetermined and unnecessary; the compact ODE will run rapidly and remain interpretable.

## Conclusion

The Chae framework can make the PPIL4 project substantially stronger because it converts the key observed disconnect—good recruitment or structural plausibility but incomplete cellular depletion—into testable mechanistic alternatives. Its immediate value is experimental design and sensitivity analysis; its later value is predicting held-out dose–time behavior and locating a recruitment, abundance, exposure, or processing bottleneck. That is a meaningful extension of `ppil4_lock_v1`, provided the docking results remain upstream hypotheses rather than fitted biochemical constants.

The Goo deep-QSP model should influence the project’s standards, not its compound rankings. Its PROTAC-specific representation, small heterogeneous dataset, random measurement split, and failed Dmax generalization make direct PPIL4 use indefensible. The most credible ISEF narrative is therefore **frozen structural triage → direct replicated PPIL4 degradation → reduced mechanistic kinetics → measured ternary parameters and selectivity → RAS-dependent downstream testing**, with each stage validated against an endpoint it can actually observe.
