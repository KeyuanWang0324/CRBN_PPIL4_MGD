# Z64 lowers GFP, but mechanism remains unresolved

- Experiment date: 2026-10-06
- Analysis date: 2026-10-07
- Date metadata added: 2026-10-08
- Data type: four sequential fluorescence reads of one 96-well plate

The pilot plate shows a **reproducible within-plate association between Z6466608628 (Z64) and lower whole-well GFP fluorescence**, most clearly at 5 µM in both 293FT-GFP and HeLa-GFP cells. However, this is not yet evidence that Z64 degrades PPIL4: the four measurements are sequential rereads of the same physical plate, treatment is confounded with plate row, and bulk GFP fluorescence combines cell number, GFP abundance per cell, optical interference, and instrument response. The strongest descriptive findings are 293FT-GFP Z64 at 5 µM, HeLa-GFP Z64 at 5 µM, 293FT-GFP Z64 at 10 µM, and an increase with the 293FT-GFP negative-control compound at 10 µM; each retains the same direction under pooled-control and same-row normalization. By contrast, the apparent HeLa-GFP decrease with TM-04-064-02 at 10 µM reverses under same-row normalization and is not robust. Z64 is supported by primary literature as a CRBN-dependent PPIL4 recruiter that produces modest, incomplete cellular PPIL4 loss in other experimental systems, whereas no traceable primary study was found that validates the exact compound name TM-04-064-02 as a cellular PPIL4 degrader. The appropriate next experiment is therefore a randomized, independently repeated dose-and-time study in which endogenous PPIL4 is measured directly and GFP is accompanied by cell-number, viability, and optical-interference controls.

## Three data levels answer different questions

The four processed workbooks contain **raw relative fluorescence units (RFU)** transcribed for each well. RFU is instrument-dependent and should not be interpreted as an absolute concentration of GFP. Across the 93 mutually usable, non-bubble wells, median raw RFU changed from **7,007 to 25,544, 23,537, and 7,857** over the four reads, even though relative well ranking remained strongly preserved: pairwise Pearson correlations were 0.981–0.988 and Spearman correlations were 0.953–0.966. This approximately 3.6-fold scale shift is consistent with a reader setting or measurement-state change, but the workbook cannot identify its cause. Plate-reader gain can alter fluorescence scale nonlinearly, which is why fixed settings, calibration, and within-read background correction are important ([González-Cebrián et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9895789/)). Raw RFU from different reads should therefore not be pooled directly.

The reported endpoint is instead **background-corrected fluorescence expressed as a percentage of the pooled no-drug control for the same cell line**. Within each read, the analysis subtracts the median of designated cell-free wells, divides each corrected treatment well by the mean corrected signal from six no-drug wells—B2–G2 for 293FT-GFP or B11–G11 for HeLa-GFP—and then averages the four sequential readings for each physical well. The final mean and sample standard deviation are calculated across four treatment wells. This pipeline is documented in [the final workbook](/Users/ryanwang/Desktop/校外/ISEF/2026-10-06_Final_Normalized_Fluorescence_Results_293FT-GFP_HeLa-GFP.xlsx), `Method!A3:C14`, with per-well values in `Per-Well Normalized!A1:G13` and summary formulas in `Final Results!A7:H12`. Independent reconstruction from the four source files’ `Long Data!A1:N97` tables reproduced all per-well normalized values to floating-point precision and every final mean and sample SD. The calculations are internally correct, although the normalized values are hardcoded rather than dynamically linked to the raw workbooks.

The **same-row analysis is a sensitivity analysis**, not a replacement dataset. It normalizes each treatment row to the single no-drug well in that row after the same background subtraction. This asks whether the conclusion survives the strong row structure in the plate, but one control well cannot define a reliable row-specific control distribution. Comparing pooled and same-row results is therefore useful for classifying findings as stable or fragile; neither approach eliminates the underlying design confounding.

The most defensible statement is that **Z64 was associated with lower background-corrected whole-well GFP fluorescence on this plate**. At 5 µM, the pooled estimate was 30.1% below control in 293FT-GFP and 24.3% below control in HeLa-GFP; same-row normalization reduced these differences to 17.0% and 15.7%, respectively, without changing their direction. The 293FT-GFP 10 µM value was nearly identical under both methods. Nevertheless, there is **no robust monotonic dose response**: in 293FT-GFP the pooled analysis makes 5 µM appear more active than 10 µM, but the difference nearly disappears after row matching, while in HeLa-GFP row matching reverses which dose has the lower mean. A DC50 or Dmax cannot be estimated from two doses on one plate.

TM does not produce a persuasive signal. Its HeLa-GFP 10 µM mean changes from 86.0% with pooled controls to 108.0% with the same-row control, a complete reversal of interpretation. The 293FT-GFP 10 µM reduction is directionally consistent but small, and the 5 µM result depends strongly on normalization. The negative-control compound also cannot be assumed to be biologically or optically inert: its 293FT-GFP 10 µM signal was elevated under both methods. Fluorescence assays can show false increases from compound autofluorescence and false decreases from absorbance, inner-filter effects, or quenching, so direction alone does not diagnose biology ([NIH Assay Guidance Manual](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK343429/)).

## Plate geometry limits biological inference

The four images are **sequential rereads of the same biological samples, not four biological replicates**. Their read times were 20:19:49, 20:22:33, 20:28:17, and 20:30:15 on 6 October 2026, while recorded temperature fell from 37.0°C to 33.7°C. After per-read normalization, pairwise Pearson correlations across the 48 treatment wells were 0.806–0.905, and the median within-well CV across rereads was 7.2% (interquartile range 5.0–10.0%). These figures support reasonable measurement repeatability. They do not provide an experimental sample size of four: repeated measurement of the same units does not increase biological replication and treating it as such would constitute pseudoreplication ([Lazic et al., PLOS Biology](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.2005282)). The present study is one experimental run with four technical wells per condition.

The largest QC limitation is that **each treatment–dose combination occupies exactly one row**, while control fluorescence itself varies systematically by row. Averaged across reads, 293FT-GFP control wells span 84.6–115.5% and HeLa-GFP controls span 81.1–120.9% of their pooled means. Consequently, a drug effect cannot be fully separated from row position, dispensing sequence, local cell density, or reader geometry. The 293FT-GFP Z64 5 µM wells increase monotonically from 57.4% at B3 to 80.2% at B6, and across all six 293FT-GFP conditions the first treatment position averages 84.5%, versus 94.6–97.8% in the other positions. These patterns strengthen the case for a positional contribution.

HeLa-GFP is substantially less stable than 293FT-GFP. Treatment-well CVs range from 5.4% to 27.9% in HeLa-GFP, compared with 5.2–14.1% in 293FT-GFP. Individual high wells strongly influence the HeLa negative-control means: excluding F7 changes the 5 µM mean from 114.8% to 102.1%, and excluding G8 changes the 10 µM mean from 112.4% to 97.2%. These wells are not proven errors and should not be deleted after seeing the results, but their influence makes the corresponding conclusions fragile. E12–G12 were appropriately excluded because of bubbles or missing measurements; optical-assay guidance recognizes bubbles as a source of interference ([NCATS microplate guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK558077/)). A formal Z′ factor is not meaningful because the plate lacks replicated, validated high- and low-signal assay controls; Z′ requires both control distributions ([Zhang et al., 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10838414/)).

No biological p-value or confidence interval is justified from this plate. Standard tests applied to the four wells would mistake technical wells for independent biological samples and would retain the row–treatment confounding. The current values should remain descriptive pilot results, reported as **n = 4 technical wells in one experiment, with each well averaged across four sequential reads**.

## GFP cannot identify PPIL4 degradation alone

The verified endpoint is whole-well GFP fluorescence. A useful measurement model is **bulk RFU ≈ fluorescent-cell number × mean GFP signal per fluorescent cell + background and optical terms**. Lower signal can therefore arise from fewer cells, impaired cell health, reduced GFP transcription or translation, altered GFP folding or maturation, pH effects, photobleaching, direct quenching, or true loss of a GFP-linked protein. Higher signal can reflect the converse or compound autofluorescence. Studies that use GFP quantitatively either resolve fluorescence per cell or combine it with independent cell counts and viability measurements ([Soboleski et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15640280/); [Lenz et al., 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2683797/)).

The reporter construct is not documented in the supplied files. If GFP was introduced merely as a constitutive marker and is **not fused or otherwise mechanistically coupled to PPIL4**, the assay does not directly measure PPIL4 abundance at all. Even if PPIL4 is fused to GFP, fluorescence loss remains a screening signal until normalized for cell number and confirmed as protein loss. Published target-degradation reporters physically fuse the target to GFP and often use a second, co-translated fluorescent protein as an internal reference; target-linked fluorescence loss is then confirmed by immunoblot ([Ngo and Jeng, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7842662/); [Portnoff et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4820223/)).

Accordingly, the current dataset **can establish only a treatment-associated change in background-corrected whole-well GFP fluorescence relative to a specified no-drug normalization**. It cannot establish PPIL4 degradation, CRBN dependence, ubiquitination, molecular-glue action, cytotoxicity, altered proliferation, selectivity, potency, DC50, or Dmax. The hypothesis that the Z64-associated decrease reflects PPIL4/CRBN biology is plausible because of prior literature, but it is unsupported by this endpoint alone.

## Literature validates Z64, not this assay

Z64 has a strong primary-literature connection to PPIL4. Baek and colleagues screened more than 6,000 IMiD analogues and reported a **0.34 µM EC50 for Z6466608628-induced recruitment of PPIL4 to CRBN** in a purified-protein TR-FRET assay. This is a biochemical recruitment EC50, not a cellular degradation DC50, viability IC50, or expected effective concentration in 293FT-GFP or HeLa-GFP cells ([Baek et al., Nature Communications, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-62099-w)). In Kelly-cell lysate IP-MS, Z64 enriched PPIL4 approximately 16.7-fold; in MOLT4 cells treated with 1 µM Z64 for 5 hours, PPIL4 abundance was approximately 31% lower by whole-cell proteomics, with adjusted P = 0.0146 ([official Supplementary Data 5](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-025-62099-w/MediaObjects/41467_2025_62099_MOESM7_ESM.xlsx)). The authors also demonstrated dose-dependent PPIL4 depletion by western blot and time-dependent in-vitro ubiquitylation, but explicitly reported incomplete cellular degradation even at 10 µM ([Baek et al., supplementary information](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-025-62099-w/MediaObjects/41467_2025_62099_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)). These experiments make Z64 a **literature-anchored PPIL4 positive-control candidate**, but not yet a validated positive control for this GFP assay or these cell lines.

TM-04-064-02 has a weaker evidentiary position. No located peer-reviewed primary source identifies that exact name or provides a traceable cellular PPIL4 degradation experiment under it. Commercial listings attribute CRBN recruitment of PPIL4 and other proteins to TM-04-064-02 while citing Baek et al., but that exact compound name is absent from the examined article text and official supplementary datasets ([vendor listing](https://www.medchemexpress.com/tm-04-064-02.html); [Baek et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-62099-w)). It may be a vendor name for an internal compound, but that mapping requires a structure, catalog and lot documentation, or an explicit author/vendor cross-reference. TM should therefore be described as an **unvalidated test compound or comparator**, not a confirmed PPIL4 molecular glue or positive control. The identity and intended mechanism of the compound labeled “negative control” also require documentation before mechanistic conclusions can be drawn.

## Direct PPIL4 measurement should come next

The highest-priority next experiment is a **randomized dose-and-time study that measures endogenous PPIL4 directly**, while retaining GFP only as a secondary phenotypic readout. Run at least three independent cell-culture and treatment experiments on different days using separately prepared cell suspensions and drug dilutions; the independent experiment, not the technical well or reread, is the inferential unit. Within each run, test vehicle, Z64, TM, and the documented negative compound across six to eight concentrations spanning below and above the biochemical Z64 recruitment EC50, with equal final DMSO and several time points. Quantify endogenous PPIL4 by immunoblot normalized to total-protein staining or validated stable loading controls. In parallel, measure nuclei or cell count, an orthogonal viability endpoint, and GFP per viable cell by imaging or flow cytometry. This design tests whether the GFP decrease corresponds to target loss rather than a lower cell number.

Plate layout and acquisition should be repaired at the same time. Randomize or block every condition across multiple rows and columns, place several vehicle controls in every block, fix and record excitation/emission wavelengths, gain, focal height, temperature/equilibration, shaking, and read order, and use one prespecified endpoint read unless kinetics are an explicit objective. Add compound-only/no-cell wells at each dose, parental GFP-negative cells, and a GFP or fluorescein standard with compound added immediately before reading to identify autofluorescence, absorbance, or quenching. Predefine exclusions for bubbles, missing cells, dispensing failures, and detector saturation, while retaining all raw values and reasons. These practices follow official recommendations for plate-uniformity testing, distributed controls, and fluorescence counter-screens ([NCATS assay-validation guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK83783/); [NCATS fluorescence-interference guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK343429/)).

If Z64 or TM reproducibly lowers endogenous PPIL4 without proportionate loss of viable cells, the mechanism can then be tested. PPIL4 loss should be compared in CRBN-proficient and CRBN-knockout or knockdown cells, with wild-type CRBN rescue; rescue by proteasome inhibition and cullin-neddylation inhibition should be tested with appropriate inhibitor-only toxicity controls. Stable PPIL4 mRNA alongside falling protein would support post-translational loss, while a ternary-complex assay, PPIL4 ubiquitination, and quantitative proteomics would establish recruitment, productive ubiquitylation, and selectivity. This layered logic mirrors the evidence used in recent primary CRBN molecular-glue studies rather than relying on a single fluorescent endpoint ([Baek et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-62099-w); [HuR molecular-glue study, Nature, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10613-5)).

## Conclusion

This experiment has identified a useful lead, not a mechanism: **Z64 produces the most stable treatment-associated decrease in GFP fluorescence, whereas TM shows no robust response and the negative compound is not optically or biologically neutral on this plate**. The strength of the Z64 observation is that it survives two normalization schemes and appears in both cell lines; its limitation is that normalization changes the magnitude, the dose ordering is inconsistent, and one plate cannot demonstrate reproducibility.

The next decision should be based on endogenous PPIL4, not on a larger GFP-only experiment. If direct PPIL4 loss tracks Z64 concentration and time, survives correction for cell number and optical interference, and disappears when CRBN or proteasomal function is disrupted, the pilot GFP signal will become mechanistically meaningful. If those conditions fail, the same data will still have served an important purpose by revealing assay artifacts before they were mistaken for molecular-glue activity.
